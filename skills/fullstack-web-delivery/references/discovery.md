# Adaptive discovery

Use existing answers first. Ask four to six high-impact questions per round and offer useful options. A typical project may need 10-16 answers; complexity determines the actual number. The forty questions below are a pool, not a required interview.

Prioritize core value, consequential rework, and current dependencies. The user supplies business decisions and facts; inspect source, scripts, tools, and technical feasibility yourself. If the user delegates reversible choices, record the reasoned default and proceed. Commercial commitments and access authorization still need a real basis.

## Question pool

1. Q01 Is this a brand site, content site, store, booking service, admin interface, or web application? Is it new work or a redesign?
2. Q02 Who are the most important users, and what do they need to accomplish first?
3. Q03 Which result matters: inquiries, sales, registration, booking, reading, or team efficiency? What demonstrates success?
4. Q04 Which markets, languages, devices, and accessibility needs matter?
5. Q05 What are the deadline, budget, content owner, present scope, and later scope?
6. Q06 What are the three essential capabilities? Describe one complete usage scenario.
7. Q07 How many products, services, categories, and detail records exist? Which fields vary?
8. Q08 Which primary menus, secondary menus, and existing routes must be preserved?
9. Q09 Which search, filtering, comparison, downloads, booking, uploads, or multilingual features are required?
10. Q10 Who edits content, who submits records, and what can each role read or change?
11. Q11 Which reference sites or designers matter, and which specific layout, typography, or interaction qualities do you like?
12. Q12 Which tone fits the brand, and which design treatments should be avoided?
13. Q13 Which logos, colors, fonts, images, video, and brand rules are available with permission?
14. Q14 How dense should the interface be? What are the theme, navigation, and mobile priorities?
15. Q15 Which page best represents the brand, and which existing elements or component system must remain?
16. Q16 Which writing qualities or references should guide the copy? Which language and voice should it use?
17. Q17 What public company identity, operating entity, market, services, and contact details may appear?
18. Q18 Why did the company begin, whom does it serve, and which real experiences support its story?
19. Q19 Which customer cases, testimonials, credentials, certifications, metrics, and partner marks may be verified and published?
20. Q20 What are the products, service scope, process, deliverables, common questions, and prohibited claims?
21. Q21 Where should progressive disclosure, spring feedback, transitions, or scrolling narrative help the user?
22. Q22 What should each major action do, where should it lead, and how should success or failure be communicated?
23. Q23 What should each form collect, who follows up, and what receipt should the visitor receive?
24. Q24 Are there complex tables, bulk operations, drag-and-drop, long workflows, or consequential mistakes to avoid?
25. Q25 Which slow-device, low-bandwidth, keyboard, touch, and reduced-motion requirements apply?
26. Q26 Who needs an account, and which sign-in methods are appropriate?
27. Q27 What are the user, employee, administrator, organization, resource, and tenant boundaries?
28. Q28 Which database, CMS, CRM, authentication service, or APIs already exist? Who owns the data and hosting?
29. Q29 Which personal records, files, recordings, or images are stored, for how long, with what access and deletion rules?
30. Q30 Which external services have accounts and authorized access? Is this sandbox or expressly authorized real integration?
31. Q31 Are there transactions? What is sold, to which customers, and in which jurisdictions?
32. Q32 What are the currency, taxes, pricing unit, discount, renewal, and cancellation rules?
33. Q33 What are the delivery, refund, after-sales, support, and ownership rules?
34. Q34 Which portrait, recording, video, customer quotation, and publicity permissions cover the intended use?
35. Q35 Which privacy, service, refund, content-license, interpretation, and dispute terms already exist, and who owns their factual approval?
36. Q36 Which repository, stack, runtime, and systems must be preserved?
37. Q37 Which local preview pages, staff entrances, test accounts, device, and environment are required?
38. Q38 Should the agent continue after the complete plan, or wait at a specific review gate? What is already authorized?
39. Q39 Which three end-to-end flows must work, and who confirms visual direction and acceptance?
40. Q40 Which source, archive, deployment preparation, publication, maintenance, and rollback deliverables are required?

## Exit and persistence

Record each fact as user_decision, verified_fact, assumption, or pending, with its source. Stop general discovery when the plan and acceptance scenarios are actionable. Revisit a precise missing decision when its branch becomes relevant. Keep credentials out of interview and progress records.
